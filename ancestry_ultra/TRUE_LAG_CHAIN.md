# Step 4, single true-orbit implication

Write (Z_s) for the canonical singleton spacetime, vacuum on the far west and the same absolute checker on the far east. Its actual marks at age (s) are its leftmost one at (-s) and its last discrepancy from that checker at (s-2). Write (D_Q(s,x)=Z_{s+Q}(x)\mathbin{\rm xor}Z_s(x)), always for two ages of THIS orbit. Fix a supposed period (P>0) and onset (T). For every positive multiple (Q) of (P), all (D_Q(s,0)) vanish from (T) onward.

**Target chain.** Find one such (Q) and (s\ge T) for which no later white center exposes an eastern lag difference. Then the local rule gives (D_Q(u,-1)=0) for every (u\ge s): on black the head is already independent of the east, and on white its lag difference equals the east difference. Two adjacent columns now agree at every future tick. Left permutivity forces agreement successively at every site to the west, at all ages (u\ge s). At (-(s+Q)) the second row is black at its true left mark, the first blank. False. The unproved implication is the existence of that one future-mask common tail, not the final descent.

**What exact same-orbit ancestry supplies.** A two-tick vertical pair is black precisely when the current site is black and its immediate west neighbor is white. Consequently, at every physical cell, with all operations over F₂,
$$
Z_t(x-1)=Z_t(x)+Z_{t+1}(x)+Z_t(x+1)Z_{t+1}(x+1).
$$
Subtract the same identity at age (t+1). This yields the exact two-lag constraint
$$
Z_t(x)+Z_{t+1}(x)=D_2(t,x+1)+Z_{t+1}(x+2)D_2(t,x+2).
$$
In particular on an alternating true center the nearest strict-east lag-two mismatch is always at distance one or two. This closes the attempted subimplication that the true tips could force arbitrarily long *lag-two* east agreements: they cannot even force width two. It does not forbid synchronous cancellation at every later white.

For arbitrary (Q\equiv0\pmod P), the first displayed identity at the two ages also gives
$$
D_Q(t,-2)=D_Q(t,-1)+D_Q(t+1,-1)\quad(t\ge T).
$$
A run of mismatch times of the western head therefore gives its two boundary times one column farther west. The product term at the next column is the difference of the two actual vertical western-head bonds; it cannot be replaced by an independent input. Preserving this term and the actual marked prehistory is essential. Neither the existence of indefinitely many head mismatches nor the fact that every signed lag difference telescopes on each absolute time residue contradicts the two true exterior marks by itself.

**Finite form of the hard implication.** Infinite masked agreement is stronger than needed. Fix true ages s and u=s+Q of the same clock phase, and let d be the least positive western depth at which those two rows differ. It exists by the real mark at −u; in particular d≤u. For every n≥0, the two rows agree on their first n strict-west bits if and only if the next n western-head samples agree. Forward direction: in one tick equality of a prefix of n western sites, under the common center, gives equality of its first n−1 sites. Reverse direction: the new prefix and the old first bit determine the next old bit successively, since the rule is permutive in the west input. Equivalently the first different head sample occurs exactly d−1 ticks after s. For the genuine common center this is exactly the first different strict-east reading at a WHITE time (the black-time west head is already fixed). Thus it is enough to obtain one pair s,u whose first d masked readings agree; agreement for u physical ticks is a source-independent sufficient threshold. This is a strict shortening of the required recurrence, not an existence claim about such a return. The later rightmost checker mark is u−2; the outer inequality d≤u uses the western mark and cannot on its own force the eastern return.

**The eastern mark genuinely enters the paired-flip comparison.** In the displayed two-lag identity, if the left-hand side is one, exactly one of the two terms at sites x+1 and x+2 is one. At the indicated site the two consecutive time steps contain exactly one flip, since their endpoint values differ. Follow that unique flip. A step is east by one or two and forward in time by zero or one. Each flip of the canonical singleton satisfies j=t−x≥1, since all sites past its last changing checker site agree with the stationary checker at both ages. Thus the integer j never increases along this path. Once it stops dropping, every remaining step is (one east, one future), so the same normalized lane contains a flip at every remaining tick.

Every fixed right-tip lane has purely periodic history starting at age zero: in coordinates b_j(t)=Z_t(t−j) its update is b_j(t+1)=b_j(t) xor (b_(j−1)(t) or b_(j−2)(t)). The lanes j≤1 are the prescribed checker with its alternating time phase, and the triangular transition on that phase and any finite further prefix is a permutation. Its vertical flip b_j(t) xor b_(j+1)(t+1) is purely periodic as well. At age zero the true checker/vacuum representative changes at exactly x=−1. Consequently the eventual flip lane in the preceding paragraph is exactly j=1, and every flip path meets the unique true moving right frontier. This also proves that any two such infinite paths eventually merge on that frontier.

The finite sum of the positive excesses, east step length minus elapsed time step, is therefore exactly t−x−1 for the path starting at a flip (t,x). Applied to two genuine central launches t,t+Q of equal phase, their sums differ by exactly Q. They cannot make identical successor choices forever. A proof from the common future clock and the marked west that one pair of such launch paths has equal total excess would give a direct contradiction without observing every raw eastern cell. The eastern mark supplies the exact cost discrepancy; it does not yet prevent hidden choices from differing.

The paired paths have a sharper crossing rule with no exterior input. Set g_s(y)=Z_s(y) xor Z_(s+1)(y). At each successive site y send a crossing time s to s+1 exactly when g_s(y)=0 and g_(s+1)(y)=1; otherwise keep s. If the true flip arrow jumps two sites, put its first crossing at the unchanged time and its second crossing at the selected flip. This agrees with the formula: a two-site jump occurs only if D₂(s,y)=0 at the first crossed site, while D₂=1 and selects exactly one flip at the second. Each crossing map is nondecreasing, and two paths sharing a crossing follow the same maps from then on. In particular the first true canonical central flip occurs at age 1 and lies on j=1. Every later launch time from x=0, flip or not, lies above this path at every crossed site. Its j never increases and cannot cross below 1. If that j stabilizes, every step hits a rise in the flip indicator at the next site, giving persistent flips on that normalized lane; pure rim periodicity and the initial single flip force j=1. Therefore every finite interval of central times is eventually sent to ONE time by a sufficiently long actual eastern spatial prefix. For same-phase launches spaced Q ticks the earlier path has exactly Q net extra time advances over the later one before they merge.

For a permanently alternating center g_s(0)=1, the central crossing map is the identity on that entire mature interval. Thus the actual necessary Q temporal merges must occur farther east; the common wall's crossing does not create them. This identifies why conservation of the initial Q phase separation cannot be asserted beyond the wall without a new use of the marked west.

**The crossing itself can keep the arbitrary lag Q fixed.** Subtract the vertical gate at the two true ages. Every nonzero D_Q(s,x) selects exactly one different term: (a) the two one-tick flip indicators at x+1 differ, so exactly one of D_Q(s,x+1), D_Q(s+1,x+1) is one; or (b) the two true vertical black bonds at x+2 differ, so at least one of D_Q(s,x+2), D_Q(s+1,x+2) is one. In (b) choose the earliest available time. These are synchronized disagreements of the same lag Q, not discrepancies between unrelated rows. The east/time increments again lie in {1,2} and {0,1}. Refine a two-site step by a horizontal first crossing; all resulting unit crossings follow E_y(s)=s+[D_Q(s,y)=0 and D_Q(s+1,y)=1]. In (b) the first crossed site has two equal consecutive D_Q bits and the second has a discrepancy in that two-tick interval.

The later rightmost discrepancy is exactly s+Q−2. Therefore the remaining slack from this eastern mark is nonnegative and nonincreasing along the path. Its eventual constant value j has a permanently nonzero normalized D_Q lane; by the purely periodic right-rim prefixes of BOTH true ages it must already be one at age zero. The lag initially has support in [−Q,Q−2], so j≤2Q−2. In particular the path from each western mark (s,−s−Q), for every s≥T, must cross the wall and connect to the true eastern rim within that fixed collar. If the wall remains synchronized, every such path must jump from −1 directly to +1 via a different true vertical bond, at a white time v, and D_Q(v,−1)=D_Q(v,+1)=1 there. The two unit crossings through 0 and +1 both leave this timestamp v unchanged. A universal argument that at least one western-mark path cannot make this compulsory skip would give the required contradiction. No density or injectivity of the set of these arrival times is assumed: each monotone E_y can coalesce two late clocks precisely at a rise.

**The terminal lag lane is the actual eastern mark, uniquely.** In the unmodified finite singleton write B_k(t)=cell(t,t−k). The root of this triangular right-rim update is B_0=1. For k≥1, the longest constant temporal run at B_k has length exactly ceil(k/2). Its first ceil(k/2) values are zero because those sites have not entered the support. Conversely a constant run of L values forces B_(k−1) and B_(k−2) simultaneously zero for L−1 ticks. After h iterations all indices between k−2h and k−h are zero for L−h ticks. At h=ceil(k/2) this includes 0, impossible if L>h. The finite-prefix permutation extends the bound and witnesses to every cyclic phase. The canonical checker representative agrees with the raw singleton for k≥1; for k≤1 its physical bits are the fixed checker. Thus at interior k≥1 and Q≥2 the largest constant runs of B_k and B_(k+Q) have different lengths, so no time translate can agree with, or complement, the former at every phase. On the older checker side an always different later lane must have constant runs of length one, hence k+Q≤2. For k+Q≤1 both are the same physical checker, so k+Q=2. Q=1 was already settled by its solitary age-zero flip. This proves that every synchronized lag ray from every genuine western-mark launch reaches precisely x=s+Q−2 at its eventual landing age s.

At Q=2 the two western extremes themselves connect on one horizontal first jump at every positive age. The later first three west-rim bits are 1,1,0; the fourth is unnecessary. At x=−s−2 the two true ages have old/late values 0/1. At x+1, the old column is 0,1 across the two times, the later 1,0, giving identical flip indicators. At x+2=−s the old vertical bond is 1 (old rim followed by its permanent neighbor), the later bond is 0 (its old rim-depth two is white). The first differing endpoint is at time s itself, with old/late 1/0. There is no uncertainty about the direction, sign exchange, or time of this launch. The obstacle remains the obligatory white skip at the center, not the source or terminal mark.

A caution on the remaining crossing-time question has a symbolic true-source witness at Q=2. Consecutive western launches need not stay distinct even in the very first overlap cells. Write b for the later left-rim depth-three value at launch s. This lane toggles (depths one and two are permanently 1,0), and depth four is eventually 1. Both launches enter the older frontier at their original ages by the preceding jump. If b=1, the earlier launch, on crossing the first site east of its older mark, sees D change from 0 to 1 and advances to s+1. The next launch sees the complementary depth-three phase, stays at time s+1 on its own first east crossing, and sees D=1 on its next crossing. The two have then reached the same physical site x=−s+1 at the same time s+1 and share all later crossings. Any argument transferring every distinct western launch to a distinct central white tick is therefore false already at the actual marked boundaries.

For Q=2 on an alternating wall, the mandatory bonded crossing has an added exact time tag. If R_t differs from R_(t+2) at a white t, then (R_t,R_(t+1),R_(t+2)) is exactly 110 or 001. A high R_t persists over the white update. Conversely a high R_(t+2) after the intervening black requires R_(t+1)=0. In BOTH orientations g_t(1)=0 and g_(t+1)(1)=1. Thus the two adjacent true-flip clock launches t and t+1 merge at their very first eastern crossing. Neither the western launches nor the time labels on the eastern side may be counted as independent at such a skip.

The true-lag crossing has an extremal companion, derived directly from the original local update. At a disagreement (t,x), put y=x+1. If either of the two D bits at (t,y),(t+1,y) is one, we may follow ANY nonzero one as a genuine discrepant vertex. If both are zero, the two rule updates at y have common outputs and opposite west inputs, hence opposite OR inputs. The current bits at y agree, so they are white and the two bits at y+1 differ at time t. Cross y horizontally as a ghost and continue to that genuine disagreement, or to the next-time one there if also available. The earliest unit-clock rule stays E, whereas the latest is L_y(t)=t+[D_Q(t+1,y)=1]. Both are nondecreasing; a vertical 11 of actual D offers both times, whereas a 010 coalesces the two adjacent incoming clocks even under this larger choice (under the stated rule of using a genuine immediate neighbor when available). Every unbounded path so obtained still stabilizes on the same true eastern mark by the previous normalized-lane argument. In particular every one of these choices made before leaving the west must give a genuine white skip under the hypothesized period. Merger counts alone cannot contradict it: directly before a synchronized wall, if d(t)=D_Q(t,-1), the next western site is the derivative d(t) xor d(t+1); a temporal 010 of d gives a vertical pair of ones one site west, with both legal clocks then forced to its single middle white time.

For a prescribed old age s>=T and lag Q divisible by P, the westmost launch must reach its white skip by age 2s+Q-1: it has s+Q-1 sites to the western head and each adds at most one tick. Thus a fully aligned masked future of length s+Q after s suffices even without any assumed uniform maximum transit. For fixed Q, increasing the onset of a bounded common block does not reach this condition.
