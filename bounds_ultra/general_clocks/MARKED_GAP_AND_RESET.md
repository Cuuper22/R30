# Closing arbitrary factors onto cyclic clocks and locating true inverse ambiguity

Let a genuine marked finite-west cut of width a lie in a recurrent right-prefix host, with fixed periodic center. Every strobe prefix of length N physical ticks can be completed, from its terminal state back to its starting state, by at most B further physical ticks (B depends on this finite host, not N). Repeat the resulting loop. Its outgoing white observations give a temporally periodic strict-west completion by the exact westward recurrence. At the chosen temporal phase, its first N west bits coincide with the true tape at the cut: a difference at depth j first changes the west-head word at tick j−1. Thus at a black of depth d=a this completion has a spatial zero gap g immediately west of the black with g≥N−a. For a loop of physical length L=N+B' define cost min[d+max(0,L−g)] over its black depths and all time phases. These loops have cost ≤2a+B. One may substitute primitive strobe periods without increasing cost. An infinite sequence of bounded primitive periods would make the original infinite q periodic, already excluded by the marked-west two-column theorem. Conversely, periodic loops with lengths tending to infinity and bounded cost yield, by a subsequence and compactness, an infinite admissible q in the finite host whose west tape really is finite and nonzero: pass to a fixed depth d, clock phase and near-center word first, then let the following zero gap diverge. This last statement does not assert a full right-half realization.

For the finite-parent test let W be one nonzero ordinary west word, near center first. Given the preceding center and west head, the old row is computed outward by f_y(a,b)=(b,y XOR (a OR b)). In its remaining all-zero target suffix, the previous row is finite if and only if the entering pair is 00: 00 is fixed and all other pairs end in 11. The four possible earlier heads/centers passing this test form a 4-bit state computed by scanning the target from its outer mark to the center using inverse image under each f_y, starting from {00}. The true left-normalized mark has the alternating low-eight patterns 0x13/0x7b after age 2 (apply the normalized update Z→4Z XOR (2Z OR Z)). This is a rooted, rule-specific supply of alternative pasts, not an alternative singleton history: the old words need not be the true tape one tick earlier. Every whole-space predecessor of a nonconstant spatially periodic row of primitive period m has primitive period m or 2m. Choose the entering point of the right-to-left period product at a zero. The first zero sends all four states to {00,11,01}; either next letter identifies two of these, leaving at most two states. As the spatial word repeats the recurrent core of that product therefore has at most two points. Going one m-step scans this core, so an old row has period dividing 2m. The local update preserves any old period, so m divides the actual primitive old period. In particular at the east end of any whole-space prehistory of a finite marked row, the tail is either vacuum, all black, or spatially periodic of primitive period 3·2^k. A vacuum preimage tail may be all zero or all one. The nontrivial preimage of all one has spatial period 3, after which every period change is dyadic. The three-layer noreset theorem consequently forces a genuine right-tail spatial synchronizer in at least one out of every three consecutive NONCONSTANT ancestor rows of such a finite landing: the nonconstant cyclic noreset ether has odd spatial period 5, not 3. This concerns actual remote tails of complete predecessors, not the center itself.
