# Closing arbitrary factors onto cyclic clocks and locating true inverse ambiguity

Let a genuine marked finite-west cut of width a lie in a recurrent right-prefix host, with fixed periodic center. Every strobe prefix of length N physical ticks can be completed, from its terminal state back to its starting state, by at most B further physical ticks (B depends on this finite host, not N). Repeat the resulting loop. Its outgoing white observations give a temporally periodic strict-west completion by the exact westward recurrence. At the chosen temporal phase, its first N west bits coincide with the true tape at the cut: a difference at depth j first changes the west-head word at tick j−1. Thus at a black of depth d=a this completion has a spatial zero gap g immediately west of the black with g≥N−a. For a loop of physical length L=N+B' define cost min[d+max(0,L−g)] over its black depths and all time phases. These loops have cost ≤2a+B. One may substitute primitive strobe periods without increasing cost. An infinite sequence of bounded primitive periods would make the original infinite q periodic, already excluded by the marked-west two-column theorem. Conversely, periodic loops with lengths tending to infinity and bounded cost yield, by a subsequence and compactness, an infinite admissible q in the finite host whose west tape really is finite and nonzero: pass to a fixed depth d, clock phase and near-center word first, then let the following zero gap diverge. This last statement does not assert a full right-half realization.

In its remaining all-zero target suffix, the previous row is finite if and only if the entering pair is 00: 00 is fixed and all other pairs end in 11. The four possible earlier heads/centers passing this test form a 4-bit state computed by scanning the target from its outer mark to the center using inverse image under each f_y, starting from {00}. The true left-normalized mark has the alternating low-eight patterns 0x13/0x7b after age 2 (apply the normalized update Z→4Z XOR (2Z OR Z)). This is a rooted, rule-specific supply of alternative pasts, not an alternative singleton history: the old words need not be the true tape one tick earlier. Every whole-space predecessor of a nonconstant spatially periodic row of primitive period m has primitive period m or 2m. Choose the entering point of the right-to-left period product at a zero. The first zero sends all four states to {00,11,01}; either next letter identifies two of these, leaving at most two states. As the spatial word repeats the recurrent core of that product therefore has at most two points. Going one m-step scans this core, so an old row has period dividing 2m. The local update preserves any old period, so m divides the actual primitive old period. In particular at the east end of any whole-space prehistory of a finite marked row, the tail is either vacuum, all black, or spatially periodic of primitive period 3·2^k. A vacuum preimage tail may be all zero or all one. The nontrivial preimage of all one has spatial period 3, after which every period change is dyadic. ## Full-ray comparison at a finite landing

For an entire finite target Y, all k-step prehistories are indexed bijectively by the *aligned* spatially periodic elements of F^(-k)(0). A single-step right periodic tail extends left through the entire known target by the four-state scanner; its two old cells at the end of that tail set all subsequent scan states. Repeat k times. Conversely any full prehistory has exactly such an eventually periodic far-right tail; during forward replay its periodic extension maps to vacuum in k steps. This count does not depend on the finite core. Starting with the specified past centers means filtering the resulting rows on x=0; doing this does not give fresh choices for previous white reads. For the established single-step global degrees 2 on eventually white targets and 3 on eventually black targets see WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit, equivalents, Preimages (https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/main/equivalents/CATALOG.md). The frozen project already has the spatial ancestor fold rule; here it bounds joint multi-step histories of a given marked landing.

On a nonconstant ancestral tail, call a node B if it has two distinct parents; otherwise its parent is unique. Among B's two parents at least one has a repeated isolated black, hence its own parent is unique. Indeed the two old periodic configurations have disjoint blacks and no adjacent common blanks (otherwise an old pair agrees, forcing equality westward and then everywhere by periodicity). If there is no common blank at all, every adjacent pair must have the same owner (in the opposite case both ORs are true but the two old left bits differ); the landing would then have been uniform blank. Just east of a common blank, both ORs must be equal, so the next two positions belong to opposite owners. The first owner therefore has an isolated black. The three-row spatial noreset classification forbids three consecutive B nodes on any nonconstant vacuum-ancestor path: all such tails have primitive periods 3·2^j and cannot be on its only nonconstant (five-site) cycle. Thus if U denotes unary, B1 a new B and B2 a B immediately after a B, the dominating offspring matrix in (B1,B2,U) has rows (0,1,1), (0,0,2), (1,0,0), interpreting replacements by unary children with no greater future growth. The positive growth factor is the real root rho of rho^3=rho+2. A path can wait at all-zero first, then take the all-one exit (three phases of the three-site row); summing over all waiting times changes only the constant. Hence the number of entire k-tick past center-plus-q words at *any one finite landing* is O(rho^k). Rule90 has exactly 4^k total whole-space prehistories (2^k after fixing all past centers). This is not a temporal entropy bound on the union of distinct present landings.
